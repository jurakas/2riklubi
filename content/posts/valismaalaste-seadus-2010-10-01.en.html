This announcement describes the legal situation in October 2010 and is kept for archive purposes. Amounts are given in Estonian kroons, the currency at the time. The links lead to Estonian-language legal texts.

Changes to the Aliens Act

A new Aliens Act (VMS) enters into force on 1 October 2010, repealing the previous version of the Act. The main aim of the new Act is greater legal clarity. The changes are mostly clarifications or generalisations that make it easier for both those subject to the law and those applying it to find the relevant rules.

Below is a short overview of the most important changes for employers and educational institutions. This overview does not cover every change to the Aliens Act.

Short-term employment in Estonia

Short-term employment in Estonia can now also be registered for work within a youth project or programme, provided the project or programme is recognised by the Ministry of Education and Research.

Short-term employment registered within a youth project or programme is work for the purpose of voluntary service. It may last no longer than 6 months, and the general salary criterion does not apply when such employment is registered.

The procedure for short-term employment of foreign nationals in Estonia and the related application forms are set out in Regulation No. 24 of the Minister of the Interior of 14 July 2010.

Temporary residence permit for employment

The changes mainly concern foreign nationals applying for a temporary residence permit to work in research at a research and development institution. The requirements now specify that the research and development activities of the inviting institution must have been positively evaluated in at least one field.

The Act also specifies that, instead of a hosting agreement, the foreign national may submit a copy of their employment contract to the Police and Border Guard Board (PPA), provided it contains the information required in a hosting agreement.

A general requirement for everyone applying for a temporary residence permit for employment is to prove that they have actual accommodation in Estonia. A document confirming this must be attached to the application.

The list of documents required for a temporary residence permit and the processing procedure are set out in Government Regulation No. 88 of 30 June 2010.

Employer’s duty to notify

The employer must notify the Police and Border Guard Board (PPA) of:

  • any change to the conditions of employment set out in a temporary residence permit for employment, and the termination of the contract;
  • the failure to conclude, or the early termination of, the contract underlying the employment of a foreign national registered for short-term employment (NEW!);
  • the termination of a hosting agreement concluded with a foreign national (research and development institutions).

The notification procedure is set out in Regulation No. 25 of the Minister of the Interior of 14 July 2010. Under this procedure, employers must inform the PPA or the prefecture of changes to the conditions of employment set out in the residence permit, and of the termination of the contract, in writing within one week. The failure to conclude, or the early termination of, the contract with a foreign national registered for short-term employment must be reported immediately and in writing.

Applying for a temporary residence permit from within Estonia

A foreign national who is staying in Estonia legally (for example on a visa) and is applying for a residence permit to study in a state-recognised master’s or doctoral programme may now submit the application for a temporary residence permit to the Police and Border Guard Board (PPA) from within Estonia.

Temporary residence permit for study

A temporary residence permit for study may be granted for:

  • studying at a state-recognised basic school, upper secondary school or vocational school, or in a state-recognised higher-education programme at a vocational school, professional higher-education institution or university;
  • taking part in teaching practice or other educational traineeship at a state-recognised basic school or upper secondary school;
  • attending preparatory courses at a state-recognised vocational school, professional higher-education institution or university;
  • taking part in a traineeship arranged by a state-recognised vocational school, professional higher-education institution, university or international student organisation; or
  • voluntary service within a youth project or programme recognised by the Ministry of Education and Research.

A residence permit for taking part in a traineeship arranged by a state-recognised vocational school, professional higher-education institution, university or international student organisation may be granted on condition that the traineeship is related to the person’s studies and is unpaid.

A temporary residence permit for study may be applied for in order to do voluntary service within a youth project or programme recognised by the Ministry of Education and Research on the following conditions:

  • the foreign national’s activity within the youth project or programme must not be employment, and in particular the foreign national must not be paid for it;
  • the youth organisation covers the foreign national’s living costs while they are in Estonia;
  • the youth organisation has taken out an insurance policy covering all medical costs arising from the foreign national’s illness or injury; and
  • a voluntary service agreement has been concluded between the foreign national and the youth organisation (requirements for the agreement: Aliens Act § 166).

The new Act provides that educational institutions have the right to represent a minor foreign national in proceedings relating to their stay in and departure from Estonia if the foreign national’s legal representative is not in Estonia. Under the previous rules, educational institutions had this right only for foreign nationals up to the age of 15.

A foreign national who holds a temporary residence permit for study may work in Estonia without a work permit:

  • as part of a curriculum-based traineeship at a state-recognised basic school, upper secondary school or vocational school, or in a state-recognised higher-education programme at a vocational school, professional higher-education institution or university; or
  • as part of a traineeship arranged by a state-recognised vocational school, professional higher-education institution, university or international student organisation.

In addition to educational institutions, the obligations of the inviting party under the Aliens Act also apply to the institution providing the traineeship, to an international student organisation that arranged the foreign national’s traineeship in Estonia, and to a youth organisation within whose project or programme the foreign national was granted a temporary residence permit for voluntary service.

The list of documents required for a temporary residence permit and the processing procedure are set out in Government Regulation No. 88 of 30 June 2010.

Educational institution’s duty to notify

An educational institution must notify the PPA if a foreign national who holds a temporary residence permit for study:

  • does not start their studies at the prescribed time (NEW!);
  • is removed from the institution’s student register;
  • completes or discontinues their studies;
  • concludes a traineeship agreement (NEW!);
  • ends a traineeship agreement (NEW!).

The notification procedure is set out in Regulation No. 25 of the Minister of the Interior of 14 July 2010. Under this procedure, educational institutions must notify the PPA of the above circumstances immediately and in writing.

Obligations of the inviting party for employers and educational institutions

The inviting party must:

  • check that the foreign national they have invited to Estonia has a legal basis for staying in Estonia (NEW!);
  • receive the foreign national in Estonia, provide accommodation and cover the costs of the foreign national’s stay in and departure from Estonia;
  • reimburse the foreign national’s procedural costs and the costs of enforcing the obligation to leave and of detention in a deportation centre or police detention facility, if the foreign national does not bear these costs, up to a maximum of 500,000 kroons.

Processing of personal data

A natural or legal person with the obligations of an inviting party under the Aliens Act, a provider of accommodation or transport services, and a person providing housing to a foreign national may process the foreign national’s personal data, except sensitive personal data, without the foreign national’s consent and without notifying them separately, to the extent necessary to fulfil their obligations under the Aliens Act or the Obligation to Leave and Prohibition on Entry Act.

Checking validity

Just as the validity of identity documents, residence permits and rights of residence can already be checked, from 1 October 2010 the PPA website also allows you to check the validity of a foreign national’s short-term employment registration and visa.

Liability

An employer who allows a foreign national without a legal basis for working in Estonia to work is liable to a fine of up to 300 fine units (a legal person up to 50,000 kroons).

An employer who allows a foreign national to breach the conditions of employment in Estonia, including allowing them to work in a way that does not match the conditions set by their legal basis, is liable to a fine of up to 300 fine units (a legal person up to 50,000 kroons). NEW!

An employer who pays a foreign national working in Estonia less than the salary required by the Aliens Act, or does not pay them at all, is liable to a fine of up to 300 fine units (a legal person up to 50,000 kroons). NEW!

Failure to meet the duty to notify set out in the Aliens Act is punishable by a fine of up to 300 fine units (a legal person up to 50,000 kroons). NEW!
